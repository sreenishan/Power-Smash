## (a) One-page summary, in plain English

**What changed.** The spec says Phase 1 takes two to three weeks. It will not. The feasibility audit said five to six weeks for the spec as written, and the verification work since then added real work: protecting children's names, YouTube's developer rules, written permission from tournament organizers, a safer admin, and a parser that actually works on your real video titles. My honest estimate is **about 40 working days of build (eight weeks), and I would plan for 52 (about ten to eleven weeks) with a 30% cushion.**

**What does not have to wait.** Your first rupee does not depend on the video archive. The pages that sell (services, pricing, book coverage, partner with us, list your tournament, contact) are small and need no YouTube data. So the plan builds those first. If you do the Part A items in `OWNER_TODO.md` (lock down your accounts first, then the GitHub, Vercel, Supabase and YouTube starter items), the money pages can be **READY about ten working days later (two calendar weeks)**. READY is not the same as live. The public go-live (G1) also needs four things that are not mine to do: the legal pages reviewed by a lawyer, your domain verified, real prices (or your approved "on quote" wording), and a Razorpay Payment Link tested with a small real payment. If any of those are late, G1 waits for them, however finished the pages are. You can also start earning *before* the site exists, with WhatsApp, Razorpay Payment Links and your own outreach; Part B of `OWNER_TODO.md` is that track.

**The five stages.**

1. **M0, setup and a go/no-go test (about 3 days).** Your computer runs Windows 10, which the original host's tooling (Cloudflare) did not officially support. I tested that first (it passed); with Vercel the real builds run on GitHub's Linux machines, so the question matters less. If it fails there is a ready fallback (build in the cloud instead). I also read all ~1,900 of your video titles, report how many are easy and how many are messy, and write the tests the title reader must pass before I write it. *Status 2026-10-21: the Windows 10 test passed with no fallback needed; the title tests are written; reading the real titles waits for your YouTube key (OT-04). The M0 status block in section (c) lists what is done and what is waiting.*
2. **M1, the earn-first slice (about 6.5 days).** The money pages, enquiry forms with WhatsApp buttons, instant Telegram alerts for every enquiry (email alerts and the customer's confirmation email switch on once your domain is verified), an outside uptime check that messages you if the site or the enquiry form goes down, weekly encrypted backups, and an automatic rule that hides any section still waiting for something from you (a photo, the WhatsApp Channel link, a Tamil review) so no placeholder ever goes public. *Status 2026-10-21: the slice is built and was independently reviewed, but it is not deployed and the enquiry database has never been run; the M1 status block in section (c) lists what is done and what waits for you.*
3. **M2, the archive engine (about 14.5 days).** Video sync from YouTube, the title reader, player, match and tournament pages, search, a safe admin with two-step login, takedown tools and the child-safety rules.
4. **M3, admin completeness, sponsor placements, QR posters (about 10 days).** The rest of the admin from your phone, sponsor slots with click and view counts, the "send your video" and alerts forms, live page, QR posters.
5. **M4, hardening and launch checks (about 6 days).** Speed and accessibility checks, security tests, documentation with screenshots, legal review loop, launch.

**What I will not decide alone.** Children's names (the safe default hides them or marks them "do not index"), what YouTube allows us to do with parsed titles (I will draft a question for YouTube; you send it), who owns tournament footage (the plan makes written permission from the organizer part of every coverage booking and every video submission), and anything that costs money. Section "Decisions I need from you" below lists them with my recommendation.

**The biggest risks.** (1) YouTube's developer policy might not allow parsing titles into player pages (nobody can say yet). (2) Children's data law (DPDP) applies from about 13 May 2027 and the spec's "show unless a parent objects" approach probably does not fit it. (3) Tournament rights: the badminton federation's rules put webcast and film rights with the federation or the promoting association. (4) Windows 10 tooling. (5) Waiting on the owner and third parties (Razorpay KYC, domain KYC, lawyer, CA, prices).

**Facts learned in M0 that change the plan** (all verified 2026-10-21; where each is handled is in brackets):

- **Installed versions are older than the pins in `VERIFIED_FACTS.md`**, because `.npmrc` has `min-release-age=7` and several pinned versions were under 7 days old on 2026-10-20. Installed: astro 7.3.8, `@astrojs/cloudflare` 14.3.4, `@astrojs/react` 7.0.1, react and react-dom 19.3.0, tailwindcss and `@tailwindcss/vite` 4.3.3, `@lucide/astro` 1.56.0, the three Fontsource packages 5.3.0, typescript 6.0.3, `@astrojs/check` 0.9.10, vitest 5.0.4, wrangler 4.150.0, opentype.js 2.0.0, `@types/node` 24.19.2 (added because `astro check` fails without it). The age rule beats the "latest" list; re-check at each milestone (D-040).
- **`prerenderEnvironment` defaults to `workerd` in the adapter; the project sets `node`**: 15,000 pages took 97 s and 754 MB instead of 860 s and 1,455 MB, and the static output is byte-identical (D-040).
- **The file budget is a page budget** (see M2c and R-08): 15,000 pages gave 15,010 files, 75% of the Free limit (D-009 thresholds unchanged; SA-M54).
- **GitHub secret scanning and push protection are not available on a private repository on the Free plan** (they need paid GitHub Secret Protection; public repositories get them free). Source: https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/getting-started/github-security-features. The earlier "secret scanning and push protection on" claim (D-002, D-027, OT-01) is wrong for this repository. Replacement: gitleaks in CI plus a local pre-commit hook; never make the repository public to get scanning, because the test files contain real players' names (SA-M53).
- **The deploy trigger token is "Actions: write on this one repository"**, because creating a workflow dispatch event needs Actions (write) while a repository dispatch event needs Contents (write), which would also let the token push code. Source: https://docs.github.com/en/rest/authentication/permissions-required-for-fine-grained-personal-access-tokens. `deploy.yml` has no `repository_dispatch` trigger (SA-N28, OT-42, D-008).

3. **Acting on your public accounts:** writing to YouTube, Instagram, Facebook or WhatsApp; changing descriptions, titles, privacy settings; posting on the WhatsApp Channel; submitting your query to YouTube (Claude drafts, you send).
4. **Messaging anyone:** customers, organizers, sponsors, parents, YouTube, Cloudflare or Razorpay support; sending any alert or email to a real address other than yours.
5. **Deleting or destroying data:** hard deletes, dropping tables, wiping the dev project, rotating or revoking keys, force pushes, history rewrites.
6. **Publishing:** deploying to your real domain, making the repo public, switching the protected-page modes, launching the public archive (G2), publishing any protected player or minor-related page, publishing any page with a placeholder.
7. **Changing DNS, nameservers, registrar, Cloudflare or Vercel account and project settings (including connecting the domain and any Vercel plan change).**
8. **Accepting terms:** clicking agreements, consent banners, OAuth permissions or any "I agree" on your behalf.
9. **Installing or changing your machine:** downloading large files (for example Playwright browsers, about 500 MB), installing Docker, WSL2 or a Windows upgrade, adding Windows Defender exclusions, changing system settings.
10. **Running a real backfill into production** before the dry-run and sample have been approved, or restoring a production dump anywhere other than the approved one-off manual drill (D-011); production backups are never decrypted in CI.
11. **Using real people's data** in previews, seed data or screenshots.

13. **Setting any price, fee split, discount or tax rate**; it never ships a default price.
